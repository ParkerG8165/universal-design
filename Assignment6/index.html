Welcome to Colorado's 13,000-Foot Peaks

While Colorado's 54 "14ers" draw massive summer crowds, over 580 named summits rising between 13,000 and 13,999 feet offer pure mountain solitude. Known locally as "13ers," these mountains feature rugged terrain, unspoiled alpine wilderness, and world-class alpine adventures.

At 13,988 feet, Grizzly Peak stands as the highest 13er in Colorado—missing the official 14er club by just 12 vertical feet! Located in the Sawatch Range, it typifies the steep, rewarding climbs waiting for hikers willing to venture beyond the popular trade routes.

Mountain Safety & Wilderness Ethics

Climbing above treeline requires preparation and respect for mountain environments. Because 13ers see far less trail maintenance than popular 14ers, route-finding and self-reliance are critical skills.

Monsoons & Early Starts

Summer in the Colorado Rockies brings severe afternoon thunderstorm risks. Plan your ascent to reach the summit and descend below the timberline (around 11,500 to 12,000 feet) before 12:00 PM noon.

Leave No Trace (LNT)

Alpine tundra ecosystems above treeline take decades to recover when damaged. Always stay on durable surfaces like rock or snow when off-trail, pack out all trash, and give wildlife plenty of distance.

Essential Alpine Gear Checklist

Never venture into Colorado's high country without the ten essential systems. Weather can shift rapidly from 70°F and sunny to freezing rain, hail, or snow in minutes.

Key items include sturdy waterproof hiking boots, layered moisture-wicking clothing (avoid cotton), a topographical map and compass/GPS, extra water and high-calorie food, a headlamp with spare batteries, and a comprehensive first aid kit.
